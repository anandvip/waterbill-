I am writing in good faith to bring to your kind attention an apparent discrepancy that has appeared on the above-referenced water bill, and to request the Department's assistance in reconciling the same. The consumer has been cooperative throughout, has acted on every direction received from the Department's staff in due time, and has at no point attempted to mislead, delay, or evade legitimate dues. This letter is therefore neither an objection nor a complaint — it is a respectful representation seeking clarification and reconciliation.

A.Statement of Facts

  1. On or about [date of meter-reader's visit], HSVP meter-reading staff identified, during a routine reading, that the existing water meter installed at the above premises was under-reading consumption due to mechanical malfunction.
  2. The said HSVP official, in his official capacity, gave a verbal direction to the consumer to procure a new water meter and present it for inspection and certification at the HSVP office, Kurukshetra. No written report of the malfunction was issued at that time, nor was any written assessment of past consumption initiated then.
  3. Acting in good faith and in full compliance with the verbal direction of the HSVP official, the consumer procured a new meter at her own cost and presented it for inspection at the HSVP office on [date of inspection]. The new meter was approved and installed.
  4. The current and prior bills are computed on readings drawn from the new, certified meter (Old 233 → 309 → 373 KL).
  5. Notwithstanding the consumer's full compliance, an arrears amount of ₹4,556.00 has thereafter appeared on the latest printed bill, resulting in a total amount of ₹5,280.00 payable by due date and ₹5,352.00 payable after due date, without any prior written intimation regarding the period of meter malfunction, the methodology of computation, or the basis on which the back-billing has been carried out. The consumer learned of this amount only on receipt of the printed bill itself.

B.Observations Respectfully Placed Before the Department

  1. Authority works in writing. It is the consistent practice of the Department, and indeed the established procedure of any urban authority, that any fine, surcharge, or penalty levied upon a consumer must be communicated in writing and must appear on the printed bill itself. Verbal communication, while useful for operational coordination, cannot by itself create a financial liability.
  2. The arrears entry on the current bill carries no underlying written intimation. The amount of ₹4,556.00, while visible on the printed bill, has not been preceded by any separate written communication to the consumer explaining how it was arrived at, the period of meter malfunction it relates to, or the rate basis on which it has been computed. The consumer is therefore unable to verify what she is being asked to pay.
  3. Apparent discrepancy between the printed bill and the digital biller / banking-application interface. On checking the bill against the amount reflected, or not reflected, through the bill-payment biller integrated into the consumer's UPI / banking application, the figures and bill availability do not align with the printed bill of record. This raises a respectful concern that a third-party billing agent may be acting upon incorrect or unreconciled data, and possibly without the express written confirmation of the Department on each line item. It is hard to imagine that the Department itself would knowingly permit a mismatch of this nature, and the consumer therefore presumes that the Department would also wish for this to be examined and corrected.
  4. The consumer's conduct has been entirely cooperative. The new meter was procured at the consumer's own cost, presented for inspection at the Department's office as instructed, and installed without delay. All current readings are drawn from this new, certified meter. There has been no attempt at concealment, no malafide intention, and no effort to either mislead or take advantage of the system. The consumer's entire interaction with the Department has been in the spirit of cooperation.

C.Requests in Good Faith

In light of the above observations, and without raising the matter to any higher authority or forum at this stage, the consumer respectfully requests that the Department:

  1. Examine the conduct of the billing agent through whom HSVP water-bill amounts are routed to digital biller platforms and banking applications, and ensure that no amount is presented to the consumer through any such interface that has not been first reflected, in identical terms, on the printed bill of record.
  2. Furnish written confirmation of the officially billed amount as it stands on the Department's records today, including clarification of the latest printed amount of ₹5,280.00 payable by due date and ₹5,352.00 payable after due date, so that the consumer has a single, authoritative figure on the basis of which payment can be made without ambiguity.
  3. Kindly waive the late-payment surcharge in respect of the prior cycle (₹84.00) and the present cycle (₹72.00, if any), in view of (a) the absence of any prior written intimation regarding the back-billed component, (b) the non-visibility / non-fetchability of the prior bill in the UPI or banking-application biller interface added by the consumer, and (c) the consumer's continuous good-faith conduct throughout.
  4. Indicate the modality by which the consumer may promptly settle the legitimately documented current charges of ₹723.97 (water ₹603.31 + sewerage ₹120.66) for the period 17/02/2026 to 24/04/2026, while the reconciliation of the prior arrears entry is being examined.

The consumer has every confidence that this matter will be addressed at the Department's end with the same fairness and clarity that has characterised the Department's dealings in the past. The consumer expressly does not wish to escalate this matter to any higher authority or forum, and views written communication of this nature as the proper and dignified channel through which any administrative discrepancy ought to be resolved.

An early, written, and reasoned response would be most appreciated. The consumer remains willing to attend the HSVP office in person, on appointment, to inspect the relevant records and to assist in the reconciliation in any way the Department considers helpful.
